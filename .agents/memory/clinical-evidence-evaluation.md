---
name: Clinical evidence evaluation
description: Distinguish citation existence from claim support, completeness and clinical usefulness in Kustos evaluations.
---

Evaluate reference existence, claim support, evidence coverage and clinical applicability separately. Never describe an answer as clinically validated or free of hallucinations merely because its PMIDs exist and its structural checks pass.

**Why:** A real paper can be cited for the wrong subgroup or denominator. A general treatment recommendation can be incorrectly transferred to a specific surgical setting. A longer dossier can preserve details while still omitting the applicable guideline branches.

**How to apply:** Audit numeric claims with their population, numerator, denominator and time window. Compare the brief with its detailed evidence. Verify applicable guideline passages independently and distinguish evidence actually supplied to synthesis from externally available full text. Treat abstract-only recommendations cautiously; record unsupported specificity and missing material limitations instead of assuming the model read the linked source.

Keep simulated evaluations explicitly fictional and distinguish underlying pipeline/renderer tests from a signed-in end-to-end browser test. A single simulation is not clinical validation or professional sign-off.

**Why:** The user requested a realistic anaesthesiology evaluation; it must not imply that an AI assistant is a practising clinician or that one test establishes suitability for patient-specific decisions.

**How to apply:** Report what was exercised, what was not, and which findings require clinician review. Do not introduce real patient information into evaluation cases.
