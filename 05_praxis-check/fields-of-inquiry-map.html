Kustos · product research map

The test showed a presentation gap, not a source gap

A cardiac anaesthetist ran her own case question through OpenEvidence, Heidi, and the Kustos Praxis-Check. What she loved is mostly data Kustos already computes and hides. What she worried about turns out to be false. Six fields to work, ranked by what they actually cost us.

Trigger: OpenEvidence / Heidi head-to-head, Aortenbogen case Verdict: steal the presentation, keep the epistemics Owner: Josh (product) + build agents

Myth-bust first: the American-journals worry is backwards

Kustos might be skipping US journals. Kustos searches SRC:MED, which is MEDLINE/PubMed, the index run by the US National Library of Medicine. It is the same corpus OpenEvidence pulls from.

Her own Kustos dossier cites American Heart Journal, Anesthesiology, Anesthesia & Analgesia, BMC Anesthesiology. We are not missing American science. The real coverage gap is source-type, not nationality: society guidelines, review/overview pages, and very recent or ongoing work, which MEDLINE-by-citations buries. That is field F4, not a journal list.

Six fields of inquiry

F2

Tappable citations

“The reference is right there, like a journal article. I can tap it and see the study.”

What's actually trueKustos already cites inline as (PMID …), but as dead text. Every field she wants (title, journal, year, links, abstract) is already computed and dumped at the bottom in ## Quellen.

The leverExpose build_dossier's selected[] as a structured citations array in the API. Frontend turns each inline PMID into a tap target.

ROI: highest Risk: low Data exists · rendering
F3

Study-type at the claim

“I want to see what kind of study it is, so I can rank it in my head.”

What's actually trueEvidence Stufe 1/2/3 is already derived from publication type in rank.py. OE shows this as SR / RCT / Review badges. We hide it at the bottom.

The leverEmit a human study-type label (RCT / Meta-analysis / Observational / Editorial) per citation; show it inline or on tap.

ROI: high Risk: low Data exists · label + rendering
F1

Latency, 90s vs 10s

“OpenEvidence answered in ten seconds. Kustos needed ninety.”

What's actually trueOE queries a pre-cached corpus. Kustos retrieves live every time: boolean search, a 200-paper pool, per-paper enrich round-trips, one synthesis call. The slowness is the freshness.

The leverStream the answer (90s feels like 15), parallelize enrich, cache repeat questions. Do not trade away live/fresh to win a stopwatch.

ROI: high (perceived) Risk: low Backend + frontend
F4

Corpus completeness

“Are you missing sources the others have?”

What's actually trueNot US journals. Missing guidelines (ACC/AHA, EACTS/ESICM), reviews, and currency. Sorting by citation count structurally buries this year's guideline under a 2010 landmark.

The leverA guidelines pass and ongoing-trial surfacing (the Medal trial is exactly what she'd want flagged). Sequence after cited-only Quellen, or leaks get worse.

ROI: medium Risk: medium (leaks) New research · backend
F5

Format-fit by question shape

“A bit too much text.”

What's actually trueHer two questions mixed a lookup (which drug, what dose, OE's table wins) with an evidence map (where the field disagrees, Kustos wins). One format can't serve both.

The leverDetect question shape, match the format. Partly scoped already in PLAN-evidence-quality.md #3.

ROI: medium Risk: low Product · backend
F6

Identity & the guardrail

“I liked how directly OpenEvidence gave the dose.”

What's actually trueOE named a specific mg/kg dose. Kustos refuses dose-as-advice by design (its regulatory boundary). Kustos's wedge is the opposite of OE: controversy-first, full provenance, her journals, weekly push.

The callSteal OE's presentation (F1–F3). Keep Kustos's epistemics. Do not chase a funded product on speed and consensus-answers you deliberately won't give.

Strategic decision Risk: existential if ignored Founder call · governs all
