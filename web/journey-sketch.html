User-Journey Sketch  ·  Reference interaction: OpenEvidence

A phone she can ask between cases.

She is moving into cardiac anesthesia, learning a new field under time pressure. She found a VPN into OpenEvidence, and it hung with nothing loading. The interaction model is right. It has to work where that one didn't. This is the journey for the Praxis-Check on her phone.

What we borrow, what we change

OpenEvidence is the reference: ask a clinical question in plain language, get a cited, structured answer. We keep that shape. Where it failed her, and where her need is sharper than a generic answer engine, we go further.

Borrow from OpenEvidence

  • One question box, natural language.She asks in her own words. No boolean syntax, no filters. The query planner does the translation.
  • A structured, cited answer.Not a chat transcript. A document she can scan.
  • Sources as first-class citizens.Every claim points somewhere she can open.

Change go further

  • Never a blank hang.An honest, moving loading state. Her OpenEvidence session died on a page that never loaded. Trust dies there.
  • An explicit "where the literature disagrees" section.She asked for exactly this. Smoothing it into false consensus is the failure mode.
  • Evidence level and provenance set in code.Shown as chips she can audit. German. Works on the hospital network.
01–06

The journey

01
The trigger

A question forms, in the OR or in teaching

Situation A: around releasing the aortic cross-clamp, the timing of a drug and of cortisone is critical, and every anesthetist does it differently. She wonders whether there is evidence behind the variants or only habit. Situation B: a trainee asks "why do you do it this way?" and she refuses to answer "we have always done it like that."

She does
Pulls out her phone in the few minutes between cases.

Why: the tool meets an occasion, not a schedule. That is why this is the Praxis-Check and not the weekly brief.

02
The ask

She types the question in her own words

German prose, the way she would say it to a colleague. No search operators, no filters to configure, no field to leave blank.

She sees
A single, prominent question box and one button. Nothing else competing for attention.

Why: no librarian syntax. She is a clinician, not a search operator. The Hybrid planner turns her prose into the Europe PMC query behind the scenes.

03
The wait

Thirty to ninety seconds, narrated honestly

A real search plus a synthesis takes time. The question is not whether it is instant, but whether she can tell it is working.

She sees
A moving status: "Suche in Europe PMC", then "Studien werden bewertet", then "Dossier wird geschrieben".

Why: never a blank hang. This is the exact thing OpenEvidence failed at behind the VPN. The loader is a trust device, not a spinner.

04
The read

A dossier, not a chat blob

Worked example, her pilot question: corticosteroids and drug timing around the aortic cross-clamp. The dossier surfaces the SIRS trial (methylprednisolone, 7507 patients) and the DECS trial (dexamethasone, 4494 patients), and names the real split.

She sees
Kopf, then Evidenzlage, Was ist Standard, Wo die Literatur uneins ist, Offene Fragen, Quellen.

Why: the structure answers her two questions directly. "Is there evidence behind the variance?" (Evidenzlage). "Is this still standard?" (Was ist Standard). "Where is the field split?" (Kontroverse).

05
The trust

She checks the receipts

A clinician does not take an answer on faith. Credibility is something she can audit in seconds.

She sees
Evidence-level chips (Stufe 1 to 3), a VOLLTEXT or NUR ABSTRACT tag, and tappable DOIs she opens with her own hospital access.

Why: provenance is the trust currency. Nothing is asserted without a source she can open, no paywall is circumvented, and no dose is ever held up as advice.

06
The act

She answers the trainee, or reconsiders the practice

Now she can tell the junior "here is the current evidence, and here is where it is contested," instead of "we have always done it this way." Or she carries the controversy into a conversation about the variance in the OR.

She does
Quotes it, forwards it, or files it in her head for the next case.

Why: the output is usable in the moment it was needed, forwardable and honest about disagreement. That honesty is the value over "just googling."

Mobile-first, the way she will actually hold it. Real content from the pilot question. These render in the product's own colours, not the document's.

Principles, and where they come from

Access

Works where OpenEvidence didn't

Reachable on her network, fast enough, and never a dead hang. The whole case rests on this: even the workaround was dead.

Interview, F1: the VPN login worked, then the app hung on a page that never loaded.
Value

Controversy is the product, not a bug

"Wo die Literatur uneins ist" is her advantage over googling. We surface the split, we never simulate consensus.

Interview, Situation B and Lesart 3: she wants to see where the field is in tension.
Trust

Provenance is the trust currency

Evidence levels plus named sources she opens herself. Credibility she can audit, not a confident voice she has to believe.

Interview, Part 2, F4 and F5: which sources she trusts, still to confirm.
Boundary

The dosage line is sacred

It summarises what studies report about strategies, drug choice, and timing. It is never a dose lookup at the patient. The footer stays.

Interview, Lesart 4: Situation A touches drugs and timing; the line is explicit.
